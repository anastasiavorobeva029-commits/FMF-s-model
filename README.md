
Scenario 1: High Ethnic Assortativity
The ethnic assortativity level is set at 0.85, modeling a highly closed social structure with a high frequency of endogamous marriages (marriages within the same ethnic group).

Genetic Screening: Mass genetic screening programs and specialized counseling are absent (genetic_screening_rate = 0.0).

Fertility: The fertility recovery coefficient (fertility_recovery) is lowered to 0.5. This assumption reflects the negative impact of untreated FMF on the overall reproductive health of the population under conditions of limited access to colchicine therapy.

Expected Outcome:

Maintenance or increase in FMF frequency in the population due to high assortativity, which facilitates the accumulation of pathogenic alleles within closed groups.

The model should reflect low reproductive efficiency due to the lack of therapy, confirming the high disease burden on demographic indicators.

Data obtained from this run will serve as a control group for quantifying the effectiveness of interventions in subsequent scenarios.

Scenario 2: Moderate Ethnic Assortativity
Ethnic Assortativity: Moderate (ethnic_assortativity = 0.75).

Therapy: Colchicine supply is full for symptomatic patients; access to biological therapy (antibodies) is limited (coverage level 0.05).

Fertility: The fertility recovery coefficient (fertility_recovery) is set at 0.85.

Screening: Mass screening coverage is 60% of the population, with reproductive behavior changing in 30% of those screened (screening_efficiency).

Counseling/PGT: The effectiveness of genetic counseling and access to PGT is limited to 50% (pgt_efficiency) due to socioeconomic and cultural barriers.

Policy: The model assumes the exclusion of pregnancy termination practices. High-risk couples (absence of a normal 'N' allele) realize their reproductive potential naturally using pharmacological support.

Expected Outcome:

A slowdown in the growth of FMF pathogenic allele frequency in the population due to informed choices made by couples following genetic screening.

Increased diagnosis rates due to the effective implementation of screening programs.

Improved reproductive outcomes through the combination of colchicine therapy and early carrier detection, which, in the long term, reduces the burden on the healthcare system.

Scenario 3: Low Ethnic Assortativity
Ethnic Assortativity: Reduced to 0.55, reflecting a high degree of social integration and a decrease in the influence of genetic "closure" within the ethnic group.

Therapy: Access to modern biological therapy (antibodies) is expanded to 15% (bio_access_pgt_scenario = 0.15), minimizing disability among patients with resistant forms of FMF.

Fertility: The population's reproductive potential is optimized: the fertility recovery coefficient (fertility_recovery) is 0.95, which is nearly equivalent to natural population levels.

Screening: Screening covers 80% of the population, with 80% of those screened demonstrating a willingness to change reproductive behavior (screening_efficiency), indicating a high level of trust in the system.

PGT: Access to Preimplantation Genetic Testing (PGT) reaches 85% (pgt_efficiency), which eliminates a significant portion of previous social and financial barriers.

Policy: An ethical approach is maintained, excluding artificial pregnancy termination. Reproduction for high-risk couples is facilitated through PGT, allowing for the "filtering" of pathogenic allele transmission prior to embryo implantation.

Expected Outcome:

A significant decrease in FMF frequency due to the elimination of mutation transmission in high-risk couples.

Minimization of the negative impact of FMF on the population, allowing for a high level of fertility comparable to healthy population groups.

Systematic application of PGT leads to a shift in the gene pool structure, where the frequency of severe homozygous genotypes trends toward a minimum, and the disease burden is substantially reduced.
